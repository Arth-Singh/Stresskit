# July 2026 candidate frame

A code-availability census of mechanistic-interpretability papers submitted to
arXiv between 2026-07-01 and 2026-07-31. This is provenance evidence for
discovery pass 3 of the StressKit benchmark registry, extending the August 2026
census backwards by one month. It records what exists, not whether any method
works: no paper here has been run, scored, or graded.

Machine-readable frame: `july-2026-frame.json`, produced by
`july_2026_census.py`, which is parameterised by window and term set so any
month can be replayed and so this frame re-renders byte for byte from its own
raw dump. The August window and its outcome-blind pass 3b addendum are
`august-2026-frame.json` and `august-2026-pass3b.json`.

## How the frame was built

1. **Query.** The arXiv API was searched over `all:` for the fifteen pass-3
   terms inside the submission window — `mechanistic interpretability`, `sparse
   autoencoder`, `activation patching`, `activation steering`, `steering
   vector`, `circuit discovery`, `logit lens`, `linear probe`, `attribution
   graph`, `transcoder`, `crosscoder`, `causal tracing`, `refusal direction`,
   `model diffing`, `superposition` — plus the six pass-3b terms — `chain of
   thought`, `reasoning trace`, `persona`, `introspection`, `evaluation
   awareness`, `model organism`. 685 unique papers.
2. **Category filter.** Restricted to `cs.LG cs.CL cs.AI cs.CV cs.NE stat.ML
   cs.CY cs.CR cs.SE cs.IR cs.MA`, removing physics and biology hits that share
   the words `superposition` and `linear probe`. 537 remain.
3. **Signal filter.** Title or abstract must carry an interpretability term.
   358 remain.
4. **Stratification.** Tier A (102) matched at least one narrow
   mechanistic-interpretability term. Tier B (256) matched only a broad term —
   `linear probe`, `superposition`, or one of the six pass-3b terms — all of
   which are used across many fields with no interpretive intent; Tier B is
   retained as a separate stratum and is not part of the code census. 199 of
   the 256 Tier-B rows matched only pass-3b terms.
5. **Code detection.** Each Tier-A paper's arXiv HTML rendering was scanned for
   repository URLs. Every URL found was checked against the GitHub API for
   existence, license, creation date and HEAD commit. Each link was then
   classified from the sentence around it as an **authored release** (the
   paper's own artifact) or a **cited dependency** (a library the paper uses).

## Where this run differs from the August run

Three methodological points, recorded because they change counts.

- **The six pass-3b terms are stratified as broad.** Pass 3b filed its
  omitted-term hits as "new broad candidates … subject to manual
  interpretability-scope review" rather than as confirmed Tier A. None of the
  six names a mechanistic-interpretability method, so a paper matching only
  those terms lands in Tier B here. Tier A therefore stays directly comparable
  to August's 108. Unlike pass 3b, the six terms also pass through the
  title/abstract signal filter, which is what keeps the Tier-B stratum at 256
  rather than the ~350 unfiltered pass-3b rows August recorded.
- **The API caps a query at 100 rows.** `export.arxiv.org` reported
  `totalResults` of exactly 100 for every window that reached it, at every
  `start` offset. Any window that returned 100 rows was therefore halved on a
  day boundary and re-queried until every sub-window sat under the cap, and the
  sub-windows were merged. No sub-window hit the cap at single-day granularity,
  so `july-2026-frame.json` records no truncation events.
- **arXiv's own HTML chrome is excluded.** Every rendered arXiv HTML page links
  `github.com/arXiv/html_feedback` and `github.com/brucemiller/LaTeXML` in its
  footer. Read naively, those two links make every paper in the window look
  like it cites repositories. Both are excluded before authored-versus-
  dependency classification.

## Replaying the pipeline over August's own window

The same code was first run over 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-31 with only the fifteen
pass-3 terms, to check the reconstruction against the recorded frame. It found
320 unique hits (recorded: 281), 227 after the category filter (197), 178 with
an interpretability signal (158), 122 Tier A (108) and 56 Tier B (50).

The gap is almost entirely the window's own tail. `august-2026-frame.json` was
generated at `2026-08-31T14:24:29+00:00`, before its window closed: all 17 of the
Tier-A rows the replay adds are dated 2026-08-28 or later, 8 of them 2026-08-31.
Per-term counts after the category filter agree within a few hits on fourteen of
the fifteen terms (e.g. `mechanistic interpretability` 43 vs 39, `linear probe`
79 vs 72, `attribution graph` 14 vs 15).

One term does not agree: `superposition` returns 16 rows after the category
filter against the 90 recorded in August. The month's raw total for that term is
100 in the index as queried today, and four disjoint weekly sub-windows sum to
exactly 100 (16 + 25 + 27 + 32), so the cap is not truncating it. The recorded
90 is not reproducible against the index as it stands, and the Tier-B counts
that depend on it are correspondingly lower here. This is stated rather than
reconciled: the replay is evidence about the reconstruction, not a correction to
a frozen frame.

Three Tier-A rows recorded in August are absent from the replay (one now
stratifies as Tier B, two return no hit at all).

## Known limitations

- Papers with no arXiv HTML rendering are scanned from the abstract page only;
  one Tier-A paper fell into this case and may hold links visible only in the
  PDF. Code linked exclusively from an external project page or a figure is not
  detected.
- `code_promised_not_released` is detected from release-promise language in the
  page text when no repository URL is present at all. That is a weaker detector
  than August's: one July paper is filed this way against August's four, and
  some of the 38 `no_public_code_found` rows likely promise code in wording the
  pattern misses.
- `head_commit_at_discovery` is the default branch tip on the discovery date,
  not a commit the authors designated. Registry pinning re-resolves it against a
  clean clone through `pin_upstream.py`.
- The authored-versus-dependency split is a text heuristic, not a judgement by
  the authors. Release language in the sentence around the link is the primary
  signal; a repository whose name shares a content word with the paper title
  only breaks ties. Unlike August, repository creation date and star count are
  not used at all, so a paper that links its own long-lived repository with
  dependency-sounding wording is misfiled as a dependency here.
- Tier-B rows carry no code audit in this pass. August audited its retained
  Tier B in the pass-3b addendum; the equivalent for July's 256 rows has not
  been run.
- arXiv identifiers in the frame carry both `2607.` and `2608.` prefixes: the
  window is submission date, and July submissions announced in the August cycle
  take August numbers.
- The census counts release, not runnability. Whether an entrypoint executes is
  decided later by the pre-freeze smoke step, not here.
- No GitHub rate-limit event and no arXiv query truncation occurred during this
  run; both are recorded as zero in the frame's `coverage` block. Coverage of
  Tier A is complete: 102 of 102 papers audited.

### What blocks the freeze

`REGISTRY_PROTOCOL.md` requires a candidate to pass eight typed,
content-addressed pre-freeze gates in `qualification.prefreeze.json`, and that
file binds `candidate_registry_digest` over the whole of
`registry.candidates.json`. Adding any entry changes that digest, so the
qualification artifact has to be regenerated with it.

`qualify_candidates.py` exposes `scaffold`, `report` and `freeze`. `scaffold`
does produce a correct 69-record artifact with the right digest and a `pending`
row for this candidate — but it emits `pending` gates for every non-excluded row
by construction, and three committed rows (`acdc_tracr_reverse`,
`pyvene_interchange_intervention_ioi`, `mechtomo_finite_effect_map_recovery`)
carry `agent_panel` gates at `fail`, backed by recorded abstention evidence under
`benchmark/intake/`. A scaffold silently flips those three from `excluded` back
to `pending`. Nothing in the toolchain regenerates an `agent_panel` gate:
`qualify_candidates.py` only ever validates that evidence
(`_validate_agent_panel_*`), it never authors it.

Completing the freeze therefore needs the agent panel actually re-run for the new
candidate, which produces the intake opinions the gate is content-addressed
against. That is a step the discovery pass cannot self-administer, and
hand-merging the three existing evidence blobs into a scaffolded file would
defeat the point of binding them by digest. The row stays here until the panel
runs.

Two smaller steps are ready and were not applied for the same reason: the
`upstream_sources.json` row (all fields above are collected, and
`pin_upstream.py` would emit it from a checkout), and the
`artifacts/benchmark/upstream-static-audit-*.json` refresh that
`freeze_static_audit.py` produces — the latter needs every one of the pinned
upstream checkouts present locally, and all 37 pinned commits do resolve on
GitHub, so it is mechanical once the panel gate exists. The independent
per-upstream Nibi source-fetch sidecar (`jobs/upstream-source-fetch-array.slurm`)
cannot run outside the cluster and would list `sae_causal_audit` under the
existing `NIBI_AUDIT_PENDING` convention.

## What happens next

Entering the registry requires more than a public repository: a pinned commit, a
compatible license, an extractable claim with a locator, a deterministic
raw-output-to-finding map, a preregistered perturbation and null, and a passing
smoke reproduction. The 13 licensed releases are candidates for those steps, and
each one that fails a gate is recorded with its reason rather than dropped.
