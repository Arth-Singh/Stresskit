# StressKit diagnostic-battery self-calibration report v0.2

Status: frozen self-calibration of the diagnostic battery under grade rules
v0.3 and v0.4. This report measures the battery's own error rates on planted
ground truth and on degenerate finders; it contains no mechanistic
interpretability benchmark outcomes. It complements
[`CALIBRATION_REPORT_v0.1.md`](CALIBRATION_REPORT_v0.1.md), which validated
the confirmatory profile's interval in isolation and never ran the battery
itself.

## 1. What is measured

Every diagnostic card grades five checks (structural stability, claim
stability, score stability, beats random, specificity), each from a
percentile-bootstrap 95% interval over runs (`n_boot = 500`) and a
three-state decision (pass, fail, inconclusive when the interval straddles the
bar), then a letter grade. Until this report nothing had measured, end to
end:

1. whether each check's shipped interval covers the true value at the
   nominal 95% rate, at the run counts the cards actually have;
2. how often the point rule (v0.3) and the decided rule (v0.4) call a check
   wrongly;
3. how often the letter grade equals the grade the exact truths imply;
4. whether the card's confidence label predicts a wrong grade;
5. which degenerate or cheating finders the battery cannot separate from an
   honest one;
6. whether the null controls behind the specificity check still carry the
   signal they are meant to remove;
7. how much the verdict moves when only the battery seed moves.

Items 1 to 4 come from `stresskit.battery_calibration` (planted truth,
Section 2). Item 5 is `stresskit.construct_validity` (Section 7). Item 6 is
`references/null_score_leak.py` over the reference cards (Section 8). Item 7
is `references/battery_seed_sensitivity.py` (Section 9).

## 2. Planted-truth design

Findings are generated by the exact-truth structural scenarios of
`stresskit.calibration` (`StructuralScenario`) with claims drawn from K = 3
classes at a known modal probability and scores drawn from a Gaussian with a
known coefficient of variation. Every truth below is computed with the
repository's own functions (`true_mean_jaccard`,
`exact_expected_random_jaccard`), and the truth grade is `grade_checks`
applied to the exact truths, so it inherits the at-random floor by
construction. Universe N = 500, finding size k = 20 (12 for the alternate size
of `sizes_D`), score mean 1.0.

The near-stable structures are two-mode mixtures: each run is one of two
fixed sets that share m of their 20 components, drawn with probability 0.5
each, so a pair of runs has Jaccard 1 or m / (40 − m) and the pairwise
statistic has real spread. A first design used a fixed core of 19 with one
resampled component; its pairwise Jaccard was 19/21 in 480 of 481 pairs, the
bootstrap interval collapsed to a point 0.0002 away from the exact truth, and
structural coverage read zero by construction. That design was replaced
before any study ran.

"share m" is a two-mode mixture with mode probability 0.5 and m shared
components. J_two_mode is the scenario on which the percentile bootstrap
covered 86.5% at five runs in the pilot (`CALIBRATION_PILOT.md`).
claim_at_bar and spec_at_bar sit on their bars to expose tie behaviour
(spec_at_bar's null uses mode probability 0.49 because 0.5 puts the ratio
exactly on 1.5). The three spec_* cells are the cases the two grade rules
disagree on: under v0.3 a decided specificity fail costs one vote, under v0.4
it caps the letter at C. The uniform kinds have beats-random truth exactly 1
because the size-matched random null the harness conditions on is the
scenario itself.

Run counts 6, 10, 20, 40 and 100: the seed count papers report, the engine's
default `n_runs`, the smallest cards (20 to 22 runs), the modal card (45 to
52) and the largest cards (88 to 131). Per trial the real and null findings
are drawn from one seeded stream and the battery seed (Monte-Carlo random
null and every bootstrap) from a second, both derived from the master seed,
the cell identity and the trial index, so any cell can be sharded and merged
(`stresskit.calibration_merge`). Trials per cell: 2,000 at n ≤ 40 and 500 at
n = 100 for the primary study (seed 20260904); 1,000 and 250 for the
replication (seed 20260905). Coverage Monte-Carlo standard error at a true
rate of 0.95: 0.49 pp at 2,000 trials, 0.69 at 1,000, 0.97 at 500, 1.38 at
250. One trial costs 0.04 s at n = 6 and 7.5 s at n = 100 on one core.

## 3. Coverage of the shipped intervals

Primary study: 16 cells, run counts 6, 10, 20, 40, 100, 2,000 trials per cell at
n <= 40 and 500 at n = 100, master seed 20260904, 136,000 graded trials, no
errors. Replication at seed 20260905 with half the trials.
`artifacts/calibration/battery-known-truth-primary.json` and
`...-replication.json`, both listed in the frozen manifest.

Share of trials whose 95% interval contains the exact truth, pooled over the
cells where the check applies and an interval was available (primary study):

| check | n = 6 | n = 10 | n = 20 | n = 40 | n = 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| structural stability | 96.8 | 99.6 | 99.6 | 99.5 | 99.6 |
| claim stability | 45.5 | 57.9 | 75.2 | 90.3 | 96.2 |
| score stability | 59.7 | 72.6 | 82.7 | 88.6 | 92.6 |
| beats random | 90.0 | 92.7 | 92.6 | 92.1 | 91.9 |
| specificity | 98.7 | 99.4 | 99.7 | 99.6 | 99.6 |

Replication, same layout:

| check | n = 6 | n = 10 | n = 20 | n = 40 | n = 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| structural stability | 96.6 | 99.6 | 99.6 | 99.5 | 99.5 |
| claim stability | 45.7 | 58.0 | 76.1 | 90.5 | 96.2 |
| score stability | 59.8 | 72.3 | 82.6 | 89.0 | 91.4 |
| beats random | 89.8 | 92.6 | 92.4 | 92.2 | 91.6 |
| specificity | 98.6 | 99.5 | 99.7 | 99.7 | 99.5 |

Two checks are conservative (structural stability and specificity cover 96.8
to 99.7%), one is close to nominal from 10 runs on (beats random, 90 to 93%,
its interval being the Jaccard interval divided by a Monte-Carlo null treated
as a constant), and **two undercover badly at the run counts the cards
actually have**:

- **Claim stability covers 45.5% at six runs**, 57.9% at ten, 75.2% at twenty,
  and only reaches nominal at a hundred. The cause is discreteness, and it is
  visible per cell: the modal share of n runs can only be k/n, so at n = 6 a
  truth of 0.95 is not a value the statistic can take. Where the
  planted modal probability is unreachable on the lattice the interval almost
  never contains it (spec_at_bar 24%, J_two_mode 26%, J_pass_edge 26%, cv_fail_edge 26%), and where it is
  reachable coverage is fine (A_control 100%, mixed_C 98%, claim_fail_edge 86%). A card with few
  runs and a near-unanimous claim is the exact case where this bites: the six
  runs agree, the interval is a point at 1.0, and the population value is
  outside it.
- **Score stability covers 59.7% at six runs** and 92.6% at a hundred: the
  coefficient of variation of six draws is a noisy estimate of a population
  CV and the percentile bootstrap around it is too narrow.

Neither number invalidates a recorded check value; both say that the
*interval* on those two checks is not to be read as a 95% interval below
about forty runs. That matters most for the smallest cards in the
leaderboard, and it is now stated on the record rather than assumed.

### Grade accuracy

Each rule is scored against its own truth grade, the letter the exact truths
imply under that rule, so the two columns answer "does the rule return what
its own definition says it should", not "which rule is better".

| runs | v0.3 exact | v0.3 too harsh | v0.3 too generous | v0.4 exact | v0.4 too harsh | v0.4 too generous |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 82.3 | 5.3 | 12.4 | 70.2 | 20.8 | 9.0 |
| 10 | 86.4 | 4.2 | 9.4 | 68.3 | 24.9 | 6.8 |
| 20 | 88.9 | 4.1 | 7.0 | 73.6 | 20.0 | 6.3 |
| 40 | 91.3 | 3.6 | 5.1 | 78.5 | 15.2 | 6.2 |
| 100 | 92.6 | 3.3 | 4.2 | 82.6 | 11.2 | 6.2 |

The point rule is right more often; the decided rule errs, and it errs
downward. At six runs v0.4 returns a letter that is too harsh in 20.8% of
trials against 5.3% for v0.3, because a check whose interval has not resolved
does not count in its favour. That is the trade the rule makes on purpose: it
buys a letter that cannot be earned by an unresolved check, and it pays for
it by under-grading small batteries. A reader of a C on a twenty-run card
should read "not demonstrated at this run count", not "shown to be unstable".

### Does the confidence label mean anything?

Share of trials whose letter is wrong, split by the confidence the card
would have reported:

| rule | confidence | n = 6 | n = 10 | n = 20 | n = 40 | n = 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0.3 | high | 7.5 | 2.0 | 0.4 | 0.1 | 0.1 |
| v0.3 | low | 21.9 | 17.0 | 18.6 | 24.0 | 35.8 |
| v0.4 | high | 7.5 | 2.0 | 0.4 | 0.1 | 0.1 |
| v0.4 | low | 38.9 | 40.5 | 44.6 | 59.7 | 84.5 |

Yes: a high-confidence verdict is wrong in 7.5% of six-run trials and in 0.1%
from forty runs on, under either rule. The low-confidence label carries the
error, which is what it is for. Under v0.4 a low-confidence letter is wrong
more often than not at a hundred runs, and again the errors are under-grades
from checks that never resolved.

## 7. Degenerate finders

`python -m stresskit.construct_validity --markdown` runs nine finders on the
`stresskit demo` toy (200 candidate features, 8 planted) under two battery
arms (default: seeds, bootstrap and hyperparams at 8 runs each; seeds only at
16 runs), each with and without the pure-noise dataset as the null control,
and grades every cell under both rules. The frozen table is
`artifacts/self_audit/degenerate-matrix.md`; the rows that matter are the
ones the battery cannot separate from the honest finder (`demo_positive`,
the demo's top-k correlation selector on real data).

| finder | what it does | default arm, no null | default arm, null | seeds only, no null | seeds only, null |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| constant | fixed set {100..107}, ignores data and seed | A / B | B / C | A / B | B / C |
| index_ranker | first k indices, ignores data | A / B | B / C | A / B | B / C |
| planted_leak | returns the planted set on any data | A / B | B / C | A / B | B / C |
| size_inflating | planted set plus 97 fixed extras (105 of 200) | B / B | B / C | B / B | B / C |
| fixed_direction | the basis vector e₃ in R⁶⁴ | A / B | B / C | A / B | B / C |
| random_subset | seeded random k-subset, ignores data | C / C | C / C | D / D | D / D |
| random_direction | seeded random unit vector, ignores data | B / B | B / C | D / D | D / D |
| demo_positive | honest finder on real data | A / B | A / A | A / B | A / A |
| demo_on_noise | honest finder on pure noise | C / C | C / C | C / C | C / C |

Cells read "v0.3 grade / v0.4 grade".

What the table shows:

- **Without a null control the letter separates nothing.** Every
  data-ignoring finder (constant, index_ranker, planted_leak,
  fixed_direction) passes all four remaining checks with high confidence:
  under v0.3 it reads A, the same as the honest finder; under v0.4 both read
  B, because the missing null caps the letter. Eleven degenerate cells grade
  no worse than the positive control under v0.4 and eight under v0.3; the
  difference is that v0.4 pulls the honest finder down to the cheaters'
  letter instead of lifting the cheaters to A. Either way, a diagnostic
  grade produced without a null control carries no information about whether
  the method reads its data.
- **With a null control every degenerate finder is separated under both
  rules**, by one letter under v0.3 (B against A) and by two under v0.4 (C or
  D against A), because the specificity check decides against a method that
  is exactly as stable on noise as on signal (ratio 0.94 to 1.00).
- **size_inflating is caught by one check and barely.** Its 105-of-200 set
  sits on a size-matched random Jaccard of 0.356, so beats random reads 2.81
  against a bar of 3.0. Under v0.4 with no null it is not separated from the
  honest finder at all.
- **The bootstrap axis rewards finders that ignore their data.** Every
  resample runs at the base seed by design, so a data-ignoring finder repeats
  its base finding on all eight bootstrap runs: random_subset climbs from D
  (seeds only) to C and random_direction from D to B under the default arm,
  with beats random at 3.15 (inconclusive) from 36 identical pairs out of 136.
  The engine now writes a "bootstrap axis" note in that case; the grade does
  not react to the note. The seeds-only arm catches both at D.
- **The honest finder on noise never reaches A** under either rule (structural
  0.18 to 0.26, claim share 0.53 to 0.58, score CV 1.35 to 2.31).

## 8. Do the null controls still carry the signal?

The specificity check compares structural stability on real data with
stability on a null control; it never looks at the method's score on the
null, although every card records it (`metrics.null_control.score_mean`).
`references/null_score_leak.py` reads those scores for the 44 cards with a
null block (38 batteries once the six `.directions` cards, which re-express
the same runs as their base refusal cards, are counted once) and asks whether
the null still scores like the real data. Frozen output:
`artifacts/self_audit/null-score-leak.{json,md}`.

The statistic is the signed standardized difference
d = polarity · (real − null) / pooled sd, with a Welch z, a retention ratio
null / real for scores with a natural zero, and a percentile-bootstrap
interval on the difference where per-run null scores exist (20 cards). The
polarity and scale of every score are declared per card family from the
runner's score definition (the table at the end of the artifact). Classes,
thresholds being choices: the null **matches or exceeds** the real score when
d ≤ 0.5 or retention ≥ 0.9; the null is **degraded** when d ≥ 1.0, z ≥ 1.96
and retention ≤ 0.5 (or the score has no natural zero); anything else is
partial. No card fell in between.

| null family | specificity | null matches or exceeds | null degraded | batteries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| signal-destroying | pass | 2 | 11 | 13 |
| signal-destroying | undecided | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| signal-destroying | fail | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| structure-preserving | pass | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| structure-preserving | undecided | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| structure-preserving | fail | 6 | 11 | 17 |

What it says about the 17 structure-preserving specificity failures, the
ones `RESULTS.md` reads as "the method is as stable on a corrupted task as
on the real one":

- On **6** of them the null still scores as well as or better than the real
  data: CoAx (null AUC 0.955 against real 0.919, the difference's interval
  excludes zero on the wrong side), Greater-than (0.967 against 0.999, 97%
  retained), the three homonym profiles (the deranged pairing reconverges
  more than the real one) and SAE causal inertness (the permuted pairing is
  4.8x more "inert"). There the specificity failure says the null was too
  soft to be a null, not that the method is non-specific; those six cards
  should be read as untested on specificity.
- On **11** the null score collapses (Communication Map 0.045 against 0.802,
  the Jacobian lens and all nine lens baselines at 8% to 27% of the real hit
  rate) while the structure stays as stable as on real data. That is the
  reading the ledger gives: a fixed-shape ranker or census is a property of
  the method.

Soft nulls are not confined to the structure-preserving family. AMS, the one
signal-destroying null that fails specificity, keeps 94% of the real
leave-one-out accuracy after half the pair labels are swapped (0.648 against
0.690); SWD's random-token calibration blocks match the real cross-entropy
delta (interval on the difference [−0.0007, 0.0007]). The IOI cards are
classified "matches" for the wrong reason: under random answer names the
faithfulness denominator is near zero and the null score explodes (null sd
1.5 / 3.3 / 8.3 against real 0.03 to 0.05), so d is small while the null mean
is 1.35x, 0.12x and 4.16x the real one; the class is a variance artifact, not
a soft null. Sycophancy on Gemma-3-12B "matches" because the paper's own
prediction for Gemma is no transfer drop, so a null with no drop reproduces
the finding: the score cannot separate a shared representation from none.

A battery check on the null's score would need a declared score polarity on
every `Finding` and a card-schema change, so it stays an offline audit
(`METHOD_AUDIT.md` A9); `stresskit verify` cannot recompute it from a card
because per-run null scores are not on the card.

## 11. Fixes made while building this report

- Grade rule v0.4 (`CHANGELOG.md`): a check counts only when its whole
  interval clears the bar; a decided specificity fail caps at C; a battery
  without a null control caps at B; the at-random floor is a registered
  threshold. Motivated by the first row of the table above.
- `from_findings` pooled its null battery without the 2x size guard that
  `stress()` applies, so a post-hoc specificity point estimate could sit on a
  different estimand than its own interval (swd_gpt2: 1.146 on the card, 1.377
  post hoc, same interval). Fixed; the verdict trace inherits the fix.
- The seeds-axis vacuity detector compared components, claim and score but
  not the direction vector, so a direction finder with a fixed claim and score
  was flagged "ignores its seed" even when its vector changed on every seed.
  Found by the random_direction rows of the matrix; fixed.
- A bootstrap axis on which every resample returns the base finding is now
  flagged like a vacuous seeds axis.
